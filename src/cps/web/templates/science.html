{{ _("Memory fades, at its own pace") }}

{{ _("Each topic has a stability: the number of days until your chance of recalling it falls to 90 %. A shaky topic fades in days, a solid one holds for weeks.") }}

{{ _("A topic with a stability of 7 days is at {a} % after a week, {b} % after two weeks and {c} % after a month.", a=s.week[7], b=s.week[14], c=s.week[30]) }}

{{ _("The maths") }}

R(t) = (1 + f · t ⁄ S)−d

{{ _h("R is the chance of recall, t the days since you last recalled the topic, S its stability. The decay d is {d}, and f is set so that R is exactly 90 % when t = S.", d=s.decay) }}

{{ _("This is FSRS-6, an open memory model, with its 21 weights at their published defaults. On a public benchmark of about 350 million reviews it predicts recall better than FSRS-4.5, the version this project began with (log loss 0.3460 against 0.3625; lower is better).") }}

{{ _("Cramming a topic right after studying it adds almost nothing. The harder a recall that still succeeds, the more it adds; wait too long, though, and you may not recall it at all. So the app asks for a self-test when a topic's recall falls to {n} %.", n=s.target) }}

{{ _("The maths") }}

S′ = S · (1 + a · (11 − D) · S−b · (ec(1 − R) − 1))

{{ _h("After a successful recall the stability S grows. The gain is larger for an easier topic (lower difficulty D), smaller for one already solid (the S−b), and larger the lower your recall R was when you made it (the last term, which is zero at R = 100 %): that is the spacing effect, written as a formula.") }}

{{ _("The research: in 317 experiments, the gap between reviews that gave the best memory grew with how long the memory had to last (Cepeda and colleagues, 2006). In a study of more than 1,350 people, the best gap was about 20 to 40 % of the time to a test a week away, and 5 to 10 % of a year away (Cepeda and colleagues, 2008).") }}

{{ _("Checked against a search") }}

{{ _("Behind the rules sits a research planner: a search over every way of filling your free blocks (AO*, on an AND/OR graph), guided by a cost-to-reach-the-target computed by value iteration (as in SSP-MMC, Ye and colleagues, 2022), replanning after each session. On a simulated semester it saves about one session in twenty on its own measure, but it takes about half a minute where the rules take a hundredth of a second, and its reasons cannot be said in a sentence. So the app runs the rules, and the search stays as the reference that checks them.") }}
