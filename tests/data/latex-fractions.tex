Recall that a function $f:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}$ is differentiable at $c\in(a,b)$ if $\lim_{x\to c}\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$ exists. The limit, denoted by $f'(c)$, is the slope of the tangent line to the graph at $(c,f(c))$.

The derivative of a quotient is
\[\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)'=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2},\qquad \frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{x}}=\frac{x}{x+1}.\]

Ratio test: if $|u_{n+1}/u_n|\to\ell<1$, the series $\sum u_n$ converges.

The conjugate $\overline{z}$ of $z$ has the same modulus, and \underline{this word} is underlined.
